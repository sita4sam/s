EU Regulation (EU) 2022/2554

DORA

Digital Operational Resilience Act

A single rule book for digital operational resilience across the EU financial sector and its ICT service providers — translating cyber governance, incident management, resilience testing, third-party oversight, and threat intelligence into enforceable standards.

The Five Pillars of DORA
Pillar 01
🛡
ICT Risk Management
Documented framework of strategy, policy, risk assessment, continuous monitoring, and audit for managing all ICT risks across the organization.
Identification & Prevention Detection & Response Backup & Recovery RTO / RPO targets
Pillar 02
🚨
ICT Incident Management
Standardised classification and major incident reporting to national authorities, with root-cause analysis and evidence-based remediation.
Major incident classification Regulator reporting Blast radius analysis Anomaly detection
Pillar 03
🧪
Digital Resilience Testing
Regular, structured testing of ICT systems including vulnerability assessments, network security reviews, source code analysis, and threat-led penetration testing (TLPT/TIBER-EU).
Vulnerability assessments TLPT / TIBER-EU Chaos engineering Pen testing in production
Pillar 04
🤝
ICT Third-Party Risk
Comprehensive oversight of ICT service providers including contract registers, due diligence, lead overseer audits, service-level assurance, and exit strategy planning.
Contract register Lead overseer Cloud CSP oversight Exit strategy
Pillar 05
📡
Information Sharing
Exchange of cyber threat intelligence, indicators of compromise, tactics, techniques, and procedures within trusted financial communities using Traffic Light Protocol.
FS-ISAC communities Traffic Light Protocol Threat intelligence Collective defense

Supporting Frameworks & Standards
NIS2
Network & Information Security
Underpins the cybersecurity required by DORA. DORA adds stronger resilience and continuity focus on top of NIS2 obligations.
NIST CSF
NIST Cybersecurity Framework
Organizations using NIST CSF are part-way toward DORA alignment across all pillars except information sharing.
SABSA
Security Architecture
Converts DORA requirements into business-relevant resilience attributes: availability, authenticity, integrity, auditability, containment.
TIBER-EU
Threat Intelligence Red Teaming
Structures threat-led penetration testing for selected financial entities, run on production systems under national authority oversight.
TLP
Traffic Light Protocol
Protects shared cyber threat intelligence with standardized handling rules across trusted financial communities.
3LOD
Three Lines of Defense
Business risk ownership, risk advisory, and independent audit — the governance segregation model underlying DORA compliance.
RTS/ITS
Regulatory Technical Standards
Implementing Technical Standards defining incident classification, TLPT requirements, and third-party contract clauses in detail.
Chaos Eng.
Chaos Engineering
Resilience testing by deliberately injecting failures to validate system behavior — hypothesis-driven, with kill switches and blast-radius limits.
⚡Key Technical Controls
Strong authentication & cryptographic key protection
Anomaly detection & automated network segmentation
Backup, restoration & secondary processing sites
RTO/RPO-aligned recovery objectives per criticality
Change management, patching & data integrity controls
Observability & failure instrumentation for chaos testing
Kubernetes-based resilience pipelines (e.g. Testkube)

📅DORA Timeline
1
December 2022
DORA published in the Official Journal of the EU as Regulation (EU) 2022/2554. Enters into force 20 days later.
2
2023 — 2024
ESAs publish Regulatory Technical Standards and Implementing Technical Standards for incident classification, TLPT, and third-party contracts.
3
January 2025
DORA becomes fully applicable. All in-scope financial entities and ICT third-party providers must demonstrate compliance.
4
Ongoing
Competent authorities conduct supervisory reviews. Critical ICT third-party providers subject to Lead Overseer audits and investigations.
