03 · The Playbook

5 survival decision points

Research across 200+ MGA startups identifies 5 critical junctures where successful and failed MGAs permanently diverge.

M
1–3
Niche commitment
Stay narrow. Resist every temptation to add lines. Depth beats breadth. MGAs that fail overwhelmingly do so by writing too broadly without differentiation.
✓ Stay in 1–2 lines for first 3 years
M
6–12
Pricing discipline
Walk away from underpriced business — always. Combined ratio above 110% destroys profit commission at year-end. Market share is irrelevant without margins.
✓ Price for profit first, not market share
M
12–18
Capital raise
Raise 24-month runway proactively — even when things look excellent. Profit commission almost never arrives before cash runs out on the first cycle.
✓ Stress-test runway at 130% loss ratio
M
18–30
Reserve adequacy
Reserve conservatively; accept lower early income. Underreserving to show profitability creates year-end loss development shock that permanently kills carrier trust.
✓ Set IBNR high; release as positive surprises
M
24–36
Carrier diversification
Add a 2nd carrier before you need one. Single-carrier dependency is the most catastrophic MGA failure mode. A $180M book evaporates within 90 days of MGAA termination. Never let any single carrier represent more than 60% of GWP. Maintain 2–3 active relationships at all times.
✓ Maintain 2–3 active carrier relationships at all times
⚠ Failure Trajectory
Capital exhaustion M14–20 → MGAA audit M20–28 → carrier restricts binding authority → termination notice M30–42 (90–180 day wind-down) → book transferred at pennies on the dollar. Assets sold at under 10 cents on dollar invested. Identifiable, preventable, fatal.

05 · The Destination

Path to carrier conversion

Carrier status removes fronting dependency, unlocks investment income that dwarfs commission, and creates a defensible balance sheet.

Conversion triggers — all must be met

✓
GWP $50–75M+ for 2+ consecutive years
Below this, carrier economics don't justify regulatory burden and capital outlay
✓
Loss ratio below 65% for 3+ years
Regulators and A.M. Best require demonstrated underwriting discipline before licensing
✓
NAIC RBC ratio exceeds 200%
Minimum statutory surplus: $5M–10M+ depending on domicile state
✓
Full C-suite in place
CEO / CFO / CUO / CRO / CCO — NAIC requires qualified officers in each role
✓
FCAS actuary on staff or retainer
State filings require actuarial certification of reserve adequacy each quarter-end
